# CP-OPS-004 — the trunk judges itself

Opened and closed 2026-09-22, one unit, at candidate
`98ba9bbe692724e9a2ccb3ca4e68ecbb81a1b267`. Opened because the trunk went red
the moment CP-OPS-003 integrated, on a defect that path had claimed to have
fixed without ever looking.

## What changed

One expression. `CAIRN_BASE_REF` becomes the release's own three-way form: the
target branch on a request, `github.event.before` on a push to the trunk,
`origin/master` on a push to a path branch. Only the trunk arm changes
behaviour — the other two already produced the right base.

## What it was really

Not a rule that had never run. The `comparison` rule did not exist in the
pre-1.1 checker; it arrived with the adoption. The stale base had been on the
trunk since `92f25cb` introduced the workflow and had reported green thirteen
times, each run comparing the trunk with itself, narrowing every changed-file
rule to the working tree and judging an empty set. CP-OPS-003 S01 removed the
step that was failing the job ahead of the check; what that changed is that the
check could finally speak.

## What it cost, and what it taught

Two reads by fresh contexts, ten findings, and none of them about the
expression — it was correct from the first draft and character-identical to the
release's.

Every finding was about a **claim**. The first draft invented a causal history
and shipped it inside the workflow file, where being wrong is worst. The
correction then carried a recalled figure of its own — five green runs where
there are thirteen — written seventy-six lines above the same record's rule to
print rather than remember. And `verified:` named a trunk run that did not
exist yet, in a block headed COMPLETE, which is the same shape as the outcome
CP-OPS-003 closed on.

The sequence was also followed this time: acceptance, then the administrative
commit recording `ready` at `C`, then the merge. CP-OPS-003's was written after
its request had already been merged.

## Where the correction to CP-OPS-003 lives, and why here

This path's third outcome asks that CP-OPS-003's journal entry say it was
closed before its first outcome was ever observed. That entry was appended to,
and the gate refused it:

```text
[record-integrity] atomik-project/log/2026-09-22-cp-ops-003.md is an existing
immutable record and may not be modified, renamed, or deleted; add a
superseding record instead
```

A journal entry is immutable whole — not append-only, as a step record is. So
the correction is here, in the entry that supersedes it, which is what the rule
names as the remedy. The record stands as written on the day, and what was
wrong about it stands beside it in the journal rather than inside it.

The outcome's wording was not edited to match. It is acceptance-bound text, and
this path has just spent its whole length on what happens when a claim is
adjusted to fit rather than measured.
