# CP-OPS-003 — finishing the Cairn 1.1 adoption

Opened 2026-09-21 because `37f56a5` had installed the Cairn 1.1.0 kit and left
the host on 0.2. `applyAdopt` kept every host file that already existed and
**reported** thirty-four shapes the release no longer defines, deleting none —
that is its design, and the adopter's half was not done. Closed 2026-09-22 at
candidate `8010b1cd96afababf4b7fc248e2d7d039210e301`, the second, after seven
work units.

## What changed

- **The gate is green.** The eight `tools/*.test.mjs` were the 0.2 suite and
  ran 41 of 86 red against the installed engine, inside a required status
  check. They are gone with the script and the workflow step that called them.
  A host that owns no tools owns no tool tests.
- **There is one specification.** The concept wiki left the 0.2 copy for
  `docs/concepts/`, a root this repository owns and `roots.concepts` names; 182
  broken links were repaired by where each file stands, and the four pages that
  append-only records name by exact path kept a two-line forwarding stub. The
  copy is retired behind a banner, not deleted — six of its pages are named by
  records that may not be edited.
- **The entry chain is the release.** `AGENTS.md` names the release, the six
  skills, the binding and the live view; `paths.md` is retired history, as are
  `sessions/`, `audits/` and `briefs/`, each replaced by something inside the
  path record or the request.
- **The declarations are true.** `transport.integration` moved to
  `pull-request` on a measurement — four blocking findings added, five
  refunded, all against 2026-08 integrations — and the binding stopped saying
  host protection is not claimed while the trunk was protected all along.
- **The fork is a position.** `ADR-023` records that `tools/cairn-check.mjs` is
  held one patch ahead of its release to carry two link exemptions the release
  dropped and ruled the adopter's own, while giving the adopter no field to
  declare them in. It names what retires it.
- **`staleShapes()` went 34 → 23**, and every survivor is accounted for: four
  banner-retired folders, and nineteen flat records the kit itself calls
  conforming.

## What it cost, and what it taught

Seven units, each read twice by a fresh context given only the diff and two
criteria. Eighty-odd findings; the ones that mattered were not about code.

The recurring defect was **a claim made from memory rather than measurement** —
a line count, a link count, a path id, a commit id typed by hand into a
checkpoint. Each was caught by a reader, none by a gate, and one of them had
already propagated from this repository into an upstream feedback note before
it was corrected there.

The second was **retiring a page by bannering it**. Three times a folder or a
document was marked retired while its own body, its index entry and the doors
into it went on instructing the reader — twice promising enforcement by rules
that do not exist in the engine. A retirement is a corpus-wide sweep, and the
sweep was run only after a reader asked for it.

Two upstream defects were reported rather than patched: cairn PR #23 and #25.
A third, the post-mortem that cannot count the run it prints from, was already
filed by another adopter and is named in the workflow rather than forked.

## What is left

`cairn/README.md` lists the concept indexes at their pre-S02 paths; it is
generated, and the next `npx cairn-protocol update` reconciles it. Outcome 6 of
the definition of done cites the regression as `CP-CAIRN-006 S02` where the ADR
correctly says `CP-CAIRN-001 S02`; the citation is inside acceptance-bound text
and was raised in the closing review rather than edited by its writer.
